REGULATIONS PPWR 2026

PPWR:
What food businesses need to know.

The Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation is changing the rules for packaging across the European Union. For food businesses, the transition has already begun.

01

A new framework for packaging in Europe.

Regulation (EU) 2025/40 on packaging and packaging waste — commonly known as the PPWR — creates a new harmonised framework for packaging across the EU.

The Regulation entered into force on 11 February 2025 and applies from 12 August 2026, with a number of requirements being introduced progressively over the following years.

Its scope covers the entire packaging life cycle: from design and materials to placing packaging on the market, use, collection, recycling and waste management.

02

Why does PPWR matter for food businesses?

Packaging can no longer be evaluated only on price, appearance or basic functionality.

Food manufacturers, caterers, retailers and foodservice operators increasingly need to understand the materials used in their packaging, its technical performance, chemical compliance and its future end-of-life route.

This means packaging decisions should increasingly combine three dimensions:

01

Food performance

Temperature, barrier, sealing, shelf life and food safety.

02

Material design

Material composition, weight, recyclability and resource efficiency.

03

Compliance

Documentation, substances, labelling and regulatory requirements.

03
FOOD CONTACT PACKAGING

PFAS restrictions are already relevant.

One of the most immediate PPWR requirements for the food packaging sector concerns PFAS.

From 12 August 2026, food-contact packaging containing PFAS at or above the limits established by Article 5(5) cannot be placed on the EU market, subject to the conditions set out in the Regulation.

25 ppb Any individual PFAS measured by targeted analysis
250 ppb Sum of PFAS measured by targeted analysis
50 ppm PFAS including polymeric PFAS
IMPORTANT

Packaging produced before 12 August 2026 does not automatically benefit from a stock-exhaustion period. According to European Commission guidance, food-contact packaging placed on the market after that date must comply with the applicable PFAS limits.

04

Recyclability becomes a design requirement.

PPWR moves the European packaging market toward packaging that is designed for recycling and capable of being effectively recycled within established waste streams.

The transition is progressive. Major recyclability requirements begin from 2030, followed by increasingly demanding performance requirements in later stages.

This makes packaging architecture increasingly important. Materials, coatings, laminates, adhesives, labels and other components can all influence the recyclability of the final pack.

05

Less packaging. Better packaging design.

The direction of PPWR is not simply to replace one material with another.

The objective is to reduce unnecessary packaging while maintaining the functionality required to protect the product.

For food applications this distinction matters. Packaging has to protect food, support shelf life, withstand production and logistics and help prevent food waste.

The most sustainable packaging solution is therefore not necessarily the packaging with the least material, but one that achieves the required functionality with an efficient and compliant design.

PRACTICAL CHECKLIST

What should food businesses review now?

01

02

Review food-contact compliance documentation.

03

Request appropriate information regarding PFAS compliance.

04

Understand how coatings and laminates affect the complete packaging structure.

05

Evaluate the likely recycling route for each packaging format.

06

Review whether packaging weight and volume can be reduced.

07

Verify that environmental claims can be properly substantiated.

08

Discuss future PPWR requirements with packaging suppliers.

07

Start with the application, not with the material.

PPWR makes packaging decisions more technical, but the fundamental question remains the same: what does the food application actually require?

Temperature, shelf life, barrier, sealing, logistics, presentation and end of life should be evaluated together.
