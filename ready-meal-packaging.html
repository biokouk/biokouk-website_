TECHNOLOGY FOOD PACKAGING 2026

Ready Meal Packaging:
Performance, Shelf Life & Sustainability.

Ready meal packaging has to do much more than contain food. It must perform throughout production, storage, distribution, preparation and consumption.

01

Ready meal packaging is a complete system.

The tray is only one part of the packaging decision.

A ready meal pack must work with the food, the production process, the sealing system, storage conditions, logistics and the way the consumer will ultimately prepare the meal.

A solution that performs well for a chilled meal may not necessarily be appropriate for frozen distribution, reheating or extended hot holding.

The starting point should therefore be the complete application — not simply the dimensions of the tray.

02
TEMPERATURE

Follow the complete temperature journey.

Ready meal packaging may experience several very different temperatures during its life cycle.

Depending on the application, the same pack may move from filling and sealing to chilling or freezing, transportation, refrigerated or frozen storage and finally reheating.

Packaging materials should therefore be evaluated against the actual temperature profile of the application rather than against a single temperature figure.

01

Production

Filling, sealing, cooking and cooling conditions.

02

Storage

Chilled or frozen distribution and storage.

03

Preparation

Reheating, oven use or hot service.

03
SEALING

A tray is only as good as its seal.

Seal integrity is fundamental to ready meal packaging.

The tray, sealing film, sealing equipment and production parameters must work together as a system.

Temperature, sealing time, pressure, flange condition and food contamination in the sealing area can all influence the quality and consistency of the final seal.

This is why packaging validation should take place under real production conditions whenever possible.

04
BARRIER & SHELF LIFE

Shelf life starts with the product requirements.

Shelf life is influenced by much more than the tray itself. Food formulation, hygiene, processing, temperature control, headspace and packaging barrier can all play a role.

The required oxygen and moisture barrier should therefore be defined by the food application and validated as part of the complete packaging system.

For applications using modified atmosphere packaging, gas composition, film performance, seal integrity and product characteristics need to be considered together.

IMPORTANT

Packaging alone does not determine product shelf life. Shelf-life performance should be validated for the specific food, process and storage conditions.

05
PROCESS

Cook, chill, freeze, reheat — the process defines the pack.

Ready meals can follow very different production and distribution models.

Some products are filled cold and distributed chilled. Others are cooked in the pack, rapidly chilled, frozen or reheated before service.

Each process places different mechanical and thermal demands on the packaging.

Understanding the complete process before selecting the packaging helps avoid failures later in production or distribution.

06
SUSTAINABILITY

Sustainability must work in the real food system.

Reducing packaging impact is important, but the pack must continue to protect the food effectively.

Material quantity, material structure, resource efficiency, recyclability or compostability and logistics should all be considered.

At the same time, packaging failure can contribute to product loss, food waste and additional operational impact.

A more sustainable ready meal pack is therefore not simply the one using the least material. It is one designed to deliver the required performance efficiently while supporting an appropriate end-of-life route.

Design around the meal. Then choose the packaging.

Successful ready meal packaging begins with understanding the complete application.

Food characteristics, production process, temperature, sealing, shelf life, logistics and preparation method should be evaluated together.
